## Found

1. **The weak-admixture first order is the Manakov linearization (-01, root-03).**
   - $\sigma=1$ for LP02 and LP11 is exactly the Manakov condition $C_{21}=C_{11}$. To $O(\varepsilon)$, all mean fields and fluctuation couplings coincide with those of a Manakov soliton at polarization angle $\varepsilon$. The ideal GRIN matrix differs from Manakov only in $C_{22}$ (1/2 for LP02, 3/4 for LP11).
   - By the unitary symmetry of the Manakov system, a co-launched sech pair is a rotated scalar soliton plus passive vacuum. Hence $F_{01}=F_s$, $F_m=1+O(\varepsilon^2)$ and $\rho=\varepsilon(F_s-1)/\sqrt{F_s}$ at $\delta=0$. In general, $F_m=p_mF_s+1-p_m$ and $\rho_{mn}=\sqrt{p_mp_n}(F_s-1)/\sqrt{F_mF_n}$ for every readout that treats the modes alike.
   - Derevyanko et al. (2006) use the same unitary reduction for noise-driven Manakov solitons. Rand et al. (2005) find the optimal homodyne squeezing of a Manakov soliton equals the scalar value.
   - The card's earlier item 1 ("all three are finite in this limit") was correct, but its content is the scalar problem.
2. **Walk-off is a frequency offset, including for the linearized noise (-01, root-03).**
   - In the cross-phase-only model with equal $\beta_2$, the gauge $A_m\to A_me^{-i\delta_m\tau+i\delta_m^2\zeta/2}$ removes walk-off (Sun et al. 2024, Eqs. 40–41). It maps vacuum to vacuum, and both first-order couplings are invariant. So walk-off is equivalent to a frequency-offset minority with a shifted minority filter.
   - Steiglitz & Rand set walk-off to zero and give no capture formula and no noise.
3. **Existence window (root-03).** Zitelli et al. (2021, abstract; 2024 review, full text) report that GRIN multimode solitons of non-degenerate modes need walk-off length comparable to $L_D$ ($\delta\lesssim1$). They decay towards LP01 over hundreds of nonlinear lengths and do not form for pulses longer than a few hundred femtoseconds. This may exclude the 0.4 ps lab configuration (T-20261004-claude-b-03).
4. **Photon number is not a channel for $F$ (root-03).** In the linearized model with Raman reservoir, $F$ at fixed $\zeta$ and $T_0$ is independent of photon number. GAWBS and other classical noise would grow with power.
5. **Collective coordinates (-02).**
   - Vacuum-driven noise of a two-component Manakov soliton is solved in Rand et al. (2005); ASE-driven noise in Derevyanko et al. (2006) and Doktorov & Kuten (2001).
   - The modal-partition timing jitter hypothesis is the multimode form of birefringence-mediated jitter (Mollenauer & Gordon 1994; Baker & Elgin 1998; Baker, Elgin & Harvey 1999).
   - In the cross-phase-only model, per-mode photon number and total momentum are conserved, so the energy-weighted centroid moves at exactly constant velocity $(\sum_m\Delta\beta_{1,m}N_m+\beta_2J)/N$. The passive partition jitter therefore follows from conservation laws. Its ratio to the Haus–Lai term is $3\delta^2p_1p_2$ for a bound two-mode soliton.
   - What remains distinctive: Raman- and GAWBS-driven exchange of photons between modes. Intermodal GAWBS exists for linear signals in few-mode fiber (Yoshida et al. 2025); its effect on solitons, and any intermodal Raman exchange noise, were not found.
6. **No prior quantum-noise calculation found** for a GRIN multimode fiber soliton (filtered, reference or homodyne readout), or for a bound two-component soliton with walk-off under spectral filtering.
   - Closest works: Lantz et al. (spatial vector soliton); Rand et al. (Manakov, homodyne); Lee, Lai & Malomed (vector pairs); Hirosawa et al. 2007 (soliton plus trapped pulse, photon-number correlations; abstract only); Konyukhov 2025 (homodyne, DGD-split pairs); the polarization-squeezing experiments (independent polarizations or walk-through pulses).
   - Rivera 2609.16502 has only a classical-noise example.
7. **Two phase-matched channels missing from the scope table.** 02+02↔21+21 has overlap 1/4 (LP21 is degenerate with LP02). 01+02↔11+11 has overlap 0.

**Overlap script** (sympy; $x=2r^2/w^2$; exact angular averages):

```python
import sympy as sp
x,phi=sp.symbols('x phi',positive=True)
def mode(p,l,ang):
    rad=sp.sqrt(sp.factorial(p)/sp.factorial(p+l))*x**sp.Rational(l,2)*sp.assoc_laguerre(p,l,x)*sp.exp(-x/2)
    return rad*{'0':1,'c':sp.sqrt(2)*sp.cos(l*phi),'s':sp.sqrt(2)*sp.sin(l*phi)}[ang]
M={'01':mode(0,0,'0'),'02':mode(1,0,'0'),'03':mode(2,0,'0'),'11a':mode(0,1,'c'),'11b':mode(0,1,'s'),'21c':mode(0,2,'c')}
S=lambda a,b,c,d: sp.integrate(sp.integrate(M[a]*M[b]*M[c]*M[d],(phi,0,2*sp.pi))/(2*sp.pi),(x,0,sp.oo))
# ratios to S('01','01','01','01'): 0101/0202 1/2, 02 self 1/2, 0101/11a11a 1/2, 11a self 3/4,
# 11a/11b 1/4, 01-03-02-02 1/4, 01-02-11a-11a 0, 02-02-21c-21c 1/4
```
