## Found

All findings are within the pure-Kerr, cross-phase-only model with linearized noise.

1. **"GRIN can never beat LP01-only" is false.**
   - **Fixed filter.** It fails at every $\zeta$ studied (0.5–8).
   - **Optimal filter, at $\delta=0$.** It fails for $\zeta<\zeta_c=2.67$. Converged counterexample: $\zeta=2$, GRIN LP01/LP02, $p_2=0.75$; $\min F$ is 0.62420 against 0.63475 for LP01 ($-1.05\times10^{-2}$, $-0.073$ dB). It is identical in four filter classes and stable under step, sampling and window changes.
2. **The Manakov point is a stationary point of $F$ in overlap space.**
   - At the $\gamma_{\rm eff}$ energy there is no first-order term: the odd part in $\varepsilon$ scales as $\varepsilon^{3.00}$.
   - The difference is second order: $\Delta F=K\|q\|^2+W\|r\|^2$, with $\|q\|^2=\varepsilon^2p_1p_2^3$ and $\|r\|^2=\delta^2p_1p_2$ for GRIN LP02. Measured exponents: 3 in $p_2$, 2 in $\delta$.
3. **$K^*$, at the LP01-optimal filter, changes sign at $\zeta_c=2.67$.** Values: $-0.38$ at $\zeta=2$, $+3.3$ at $\zeta=5$. $W^*>0$ for $\zeta\le5$ (symmetric optimal filters).
   - The advantage is at most about $10^{-2}$ in $F$.
   - Walk-off removes it for $\delta>\delta_c=\varepsilon p_2\sqrt{-K^*/W^*}$.
   - In ideal GIF625 at 1550 nm, the two-mode model holds only for $\delta_{\rm LP02}\gtrsim0.56$. There the advantage is at most about 0.3% in $F$, which is not measurable.
4. **Universality of $K$.** The held-out structures had the predicted sign everywhere: off-diagonal defects, a PM-fiber matrix, LP11, three modes. But the frozen quantitative tolerances failed in most cases.
   - Third-order terms are about $1.2|\Delta|$ relative (off-diagonal), plus an $\varepsilon\delta^2$ cross term ($-1.15\,\varepsilon\delta^2p_1^2p_2^2$ at $\zeta=2$).
   - The post-hoc scaling shows the ratio to the second-order prediction tends to 1 as the defects shrink.
6. **Mode-resolved weighted detection** (outside the claim's readout class, labelled separately). At $\zeta=2$, $\delta=0$, $p_2=0.75$, a GRIN launch reaches $R=0.566$ against LP01's 0.635 ($-0.50$ dB). It is still second order in the defect. The Manakov control gives exactly the LP01 value. At $\zeta=5$ there is no advantage. With walk-off it disappears: $-0.004$ dB for LP02 at $\delta=0.3$, and $+0.18$ dB worse than LP01 for LP11 at the lab pulse ($\delta=0.59$), both at $\zeta=2$.
5. **Normalization.** At equal photon number, the mixed launch follows the scalar at soliton order $N^2=\Sigma$, a first-order energy effect. This is why the comparison is made at the $\gamma_{\rm eff}$ energy.
