The grid covers $\zeta=2$ with $p_2\in\{0.05,0.15,0.3\}$ and $\delta\in\{0,0.47,0.94\}$, plus $\zeta=5$ with $p_2\in\{0.15,0.3\}$ and $\delta\in\{0,0.94\}$.

## Found

- **$\delta=0$, Manakov null.** It holds closely.
  - At $\zeta=2$ the GRIN launch is 0.004–0.016 dB quieter than LP01.
  - At $\zeta=5$ it is 0.03–0.31 dB noisier ($p_2=0.3$), and about 7 times less at $p_2=0.15$.
- **Walk-off traps the two components.**
  - At $\zeta=5$ the carriers sit at $+p_2\delta$ and $-p_1\delta$, from momentum conservation plus equal group velocity; the centroids match to 0.003.
  - A narrow band on the launch carrier cuts both trapped spectra: up to +3.6 dB at $p_2=0.3$.
  - With bands covering the trapped spectra the penalty at equal band falls to at most 0.16 dB.
  - At $p_2=0.15$ with the widest band, $F$ is 0.125 dB below LP01 at equal band, but LP01 is 4.3 dB from its own best band there.
- **Best band** (minimum over the scanned edges; the card's quantity):
  - walk-off costs 0.40 dB at $\zeta=2$, and 1.7 dB ($p_2=0.15$) to 2.5 dB ($p_2=0.3$) at $\zeta=5$;
  - the GRIN launch beats LP01 by at most 0.014 dB.
  - I first read the equal-band $-0.125$ dB as a multimode advantage, then caught that LP01 was far from its own best band at that edge. The note now leads with the best-band comparison.
- **Bands centred on the trapped carriers make things worse**, by 1.0–2.3 dB except at the narrowest half-width.
- **Mode-resolved correlation** between LP01 and LP02 counts is sizeable ($-0.10$ to $-0.57$) and tracks the Manakov rotation within 2–6% at $\delta=0$.
