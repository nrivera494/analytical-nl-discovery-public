## Model and measurement

Write $\tau=t/T_0$, $\xi=z/L_D$, $L_D=T_0^2/|\beta_2|$, $q=Nu$, and
$u(0,\tau)=\operatorname{sech}\tau$ before a phase-only spectral mask.
The ideal model is

$$\partial_\xi u={i\over2}\partial_\tau^2u+iN^2u\left[(1-f_R)|u|^2+f_R h*|u|^2\right],$$

with $f_R=0.18$, Blow–Wood times 12.2 and 32 fs, and unit-integral causal $h$.
The analytic Fourier response avoids coarse sampled-kernel normalization.
The initial energy is $E_{\rm laser}=2N^2|\beta_2|/(\gamma T_0)$ and is unchanged
by the phase mask. The physical examples use $\beta_2=-21.7$ ps²/km,
$\gamma=1.3$ W$^{-1}$km$^{-1}$, and a 1550-nm carrier.

For an isolated leading peak, independently measure

$$f_{\rm gate}={\int_G|u|^2d\tau\over\int|u(0)|^2d\tau},\qquad
f_{\rm ZS}={4\operatorname{Im}\zeta_1\over N^2\int|u(0)|^2d\tau}
={2\operatorname{Im}\zeta_1\over N^2}.$$

The gate half-width is $\max[1,8/\max_G|q|]$ in $T_0$ units. The local carrier
is removed, then a two-sided shooting calculation finds the bound state of
$\psi'=\begin{pmatrix}-i\zeta&q\\-q^*&i\zeta\end{pmatrix}\psi$.
The final fraction is checked against gate size, shooting resolution, and
other output peaks. The ordinary gated energy includes a small nonsolitonic
pedestal; it is not equated to the discrete ZS mass by definition.

With frequency-dependent dispersion, scale $q$ by
$\sqrt{\kappa/D}$ and multiply the ZS mass by $D/\kappa$, where
$D=|\beta_2(\omega_c)|/|\beta_2(\omega_0)|$ and
$\kappa=\gamma(\omega_c)/\gamma(\omega_0)$. This is a local soliton diagnostic,
not an exact inverse-scattering decomposition of the perturbed GNLSE.
The direct time-gated energy is the primary material-model energy observable.
Physical photon counts weight spectral energy by $1/(\hbar\omega)$; the
constant-gamma, no-shock ideal model does not conserve that physical count.

## Independent adjoint

For each constant-potential ZS segment, $T_j=\exp(M_j\Delta\tau)$ is evaluated
using $M_j^2=-(\zeta^2+|q_j|^2)I$. Forward and backward products give derivatives
of the outgoing scattering amplitude $a$. The simple-root identity
$d\zeta=-da/(\partial_\zeta a)$ gives the derivative of the discrete ZS
objective. The derivative of carrier removal is included. The fixed peak
selection is locally differentiable; a switch of peaks is recorded rather
than interpreted as a smooth optimum.

The Julia implementation uses the unchanged pinned solver, fixed-step
Float64 RK4IP, `adjoint_mode=:discrete_rk4ip`, sparse saved planes, and
checkpointed replay. A separate Python implementation differentiates the
scalar RK4IP stages explicitly and replays one segment at a time. The latter
is used for the larger serial search after independent finite-difference and
second-solver checks. Neither implementation stores a full longitudinal
history. Stationarity means a small gradient in the declared finite basis,
not global optimality over every smooth phase mask.

The phase basis contains Legendre orders 2–31. Constant phase and linear
spectral phase are omitted because they only rotate the field and translate
its time origin in this homogeneous model. A fixed spectral-energy Gram
preconditioner changes optimization coordinates; all reported gradient norms
are transformed back to the original coefficient coordinates in radians.
The target is $\|\nabla_c f_1\|_2\le10^{-5}$. The stronger polishing stop at
$10^{-6}$ leaves room for the independent finer-discretization check. A
callback stop with this norm is distinguished from the library's success
flag; conversely, an iteration limit never counts as stationarity.

The original searches use a coarse ZS transfer spacing. Their final masks
are independently propagated and measured before comparison, so the apparent
increase from refining only the readout is not attributed to optimization.
The three-start spreads below refer to finite-effort endpoints, not three
separately converged local maxima. Only selected candidates receive polishing.

## Cheap analytical checks and scope of the bound

Parseval's identity gives exact input-energy preservation under multiplication
by $e^{i\phi}$; the numerical phase-only error is recorded separately. The
dimensionless normalization gives $\int|u(0)|^2d\tau=2$, hence the factor
$2/N^2$ in the ZS fraction. The Raman-off fundamental sech control checks
that factor as well as propagation phase. Removing an output carrier changes
only the real part of a ZS eigenvalue, which is why the fraction uses its
imaginary part.

The quoted 200-fs conjectured headroom ceiling of 1.68 allows a soliton
fraction about 1.082 when multiplied by the independently measured TL
fraction. It is therefore weaker than the total-energy ceiling at that point.
At 100 fs the quoted 1.56 ceiling is nontrivial, and the present searches do
not certify it. Passing a finite collection of searches is not a proof of
either a sharp upper bound or a universal dependence on N.

### Meaning of a duration optimum

The TL duration scan holds $N$ fixed, as in the proposed TL peak law. Its laser
energy therefore varies as $1/T_0$. Each masked/TL comparison at a specified
$N,T_0$ uses exactly the same laser spectrum and energy. The separate duration
robustness test instead holds laser energy, physical fibre length and physical
mask fixed, so $N=N_{\rm ref}\sqrt{T_0/T_{\rm ref}}$. These are different
experiments. No joint optimum over pulse duration, laser energy and target
red shift is asserted.
