peak-position approximations survive as finite numerical descriptions. The
scaled share drifts with order; the energy-maximum corollary is false; and

no new claim for that failed hypothesis; the subsequent phase-2 material
prediction and failed null are recorded on C-20261017-gpt-c-01. The completed core scans and numerical
controls, retained failures and explicitly deferred cases are recorded below.

## Model, extraction and uncertainty

The reference equation is the scalar deterministic, constant-beta2 GNLSE with
exact Fourier-domain delayed response, no shock and no loss. The BW response
has $(\tau_1,\tau_2,f_R)=(12.2\,{\rm fs},32\,{\rm fs},0.18)$; HC uses the
published thirteen-mode response with $f_R=0.18$; the As-like oscillator
uses $(15.5\,{\rm fs},230.5\,{\rm fs},0.10)$.
The As-like comparison changes only the response, not the dispersion/nonlinearity
to those of a real chalcogenide fibre. HC has no unique tau1: its plotted y
uses 12.2 fs as an explicitly declared silica reference time.

For $q=N\,\mathrm{sech}(t)$, launch norm is $2N^2$. For
$q=N\exp(-t^2/2)$, it is $\sqrt\pi N^2$.
The main shape comparison holds peak-based N fixed and optimizes duration
separately; it does not hold launch energy fixed. A separate frozen control
design matches input energy, intensity FWHM and physical output length. Its
conversion between width/order conventions is stated in predictions.md.
Execution coverage is recorded in inventory.json; unrun controls are not
counted as evidence.

Leading-pulse identification uses the most red-shifted resolved ZS root and
cross-checks it against the largest soliton share. Temporal gate edges,
ZS/gated energy agreement, norm or loss-corrected photon drift, and monitored
time/frequency edge occupancy must pass the preregistered gates. Early
unseparated checkpoints and contaminated later ones remain recorded and are
excluded. Sparse checkpoints are stored outside git; no complete z histories
are retained in RAM. Every propagation checks free memory first and scans
run serially with single-thread numerical libraries.

A maximum requires samples on both sides at a common output checkpoint.
Three-point quadratic fits in log-duration and ordinary duration are compared.
Full neighbor brackets describe resolution, not statistical confidence bounds.
Regressions report their residual-based uncertainty and independent bracket
sensitivity. Numerical integration, shooting, grid, kernel and output-distance
errors are separate. A finite numerical fit is not an asymptotic theorem.

The width comparison distinguishes a fitted sech intensity width, interpolated
FWHM/1.762747, and the width inferred from the total-gamma soliton area law.
The electronic-gamma convention is also shown explicitly; multiplication of
the Raman-overlap factor by a constant cannot move its maximizing width.

## Exact checks and literature scope

At fixed $N$, $E_1=2N^2|\beta_2|f_1/(\gamma T_0)$, hence at a smooth
positive share maximum $E_1'=-E_1/T_0$. This exact algebra retains the energy-maximum
refutation. The matched-length realistic counterexample from the recovered
run remains valid. An efficient operating-point energy is a different objective
from maximum energy under a fixed launch resource.

The independent spectral-overlap derivation is in the run's derivation.md.
It gives
$$R(T)=\frac{\pi}{2}\int_0^\infty
\frac{x^3[-\operatorname{Im}H(x/T)]}{\sinh^2(\pi x/2)}\,dx$$
and the isolated-sech long-pulse expansion
$R(T)=8\mu_1/(15T)+O(T^{-3})$, where $\mu_1=\int_0^\infty t h(t)\,dt$.
It does not
prove a fission-width selection rule or the saturated-fission excess law.

The source ledger is SOURCES.md. The phenomenon and N=6 turnover are prior
work, as established by the authorized gpt-b review of DOI 10.1364/JOSAB.478975
(arXiv:2301.02945; full text for gpt-b, not accessed anew here). Judge 2009,
DOI 10.1364/JOSAB.26.002064, and Pant 2010, DOI 10.1364/JOSAB.27.001894,
were accessed here at abstract level only; their 18 fs finding is attributed
to gpt-b's full-text review. The equivalent overlap construction and electronic
nonlinearity convention were directly read in Judge 2010, DOI
10.1364/OE.18.014960, full text, selected theory section. No new claim of
novelty for the overlap integral or its maximum is made. The permitted
review also identifies a first-compression scaling objection from Tikan et al.,
arXiv:1701.08527 (full text for gpt-b; not accessed here). Accordingly,
$N^2\tau_1/T_0$ is used as an empirical coordinate; this audit does not identify
$N^2/T_0$ with a proved first-compression bandwidth or verify that mechanism.

## Peak-share and position results

All new numbers below belong to run `2026-10-17-gpt-c-raman-sharpening`.
The complete per-order values, duration brackets, output checkpoints and
resolution estimates are in the run's `tables.md`; machine-readable results
are in `final_metrics.json` and `final_position.json`.

The surviving small-order approximation is
$$f_1^*\simeq\frac{1.29}{\sqrt N}\quad(N=3,\ldots,10),\qquad
T_0^*\simeq21\,{\rm fs}\,N^{1.62}.$$
This is a finite numerical approximation in the reference BW/sech model.
It is not a constant scaled share through arbitrary order. The scaled share
rises from about 1.267 at N=3 to 1.295 at N=5, then falls to 1.222 at N=16.
The three-parameter curve used in the figure describes that measured drift;
it is not assigned a physical asymptotic remainder or proposed as a new law.

Before any holdout, the fitted guide and position coefficients were committed
in `abe44ab382621ff706ca234ac7bac8fca06d6b02`. The four interleaved heldout
orders N=9,11,13,15 all pass: maximum absolute scaled-share error is 0.001211
against tolerance 0.006, and maximum central position error is 0.869% against
5%. The numerical share envelopes and **all four full refined duration
brackets** also lie inside their respective frozen tolerances. The original
10% position tolerance contains every BW peak bracket, including calibration
orders. Midpoint refinement supersedes the coarse initial brackets without
changing any raw output or refitting the frozen guide. These are interpolation
tests within the calibration range, not extrapolation to unbounded N.

That frozen position regression is $T_0^*/\tau_1=A N^p$, with
$A=1.71940$, $p=1.62234$. Its residual-based 95% intervals are
$A\in[1.68339,1.75617]$ and $p\in[1.61215,1.63253]$; their covariance is
retained. Allowing every initial peak to vary independently throughout its
full bracket instead gives $p\in[1.47274,1.76726]$. These broader bounds
show the resolution limit hidden by regression residuals. The final,
post-test all-order and per-response fits are diagnostic updates in
final_position.json, not refits of the frozen holdout predictions.
The final all-order fit gives $p=1.62232$, residual-based 95% interval
$[1.61459,1.63005]$, and full-bracket sensitivity $[1.48516,1.75714]$.
Equivalently, the measured finite-range $y^*$ exponent is $2-p=0.37768$,
with residual-based interval $[0.36995,0.38541]$.

The direct fit of $y^*=N^2\tau_1/T_0^*$ is reported as $y^*=A_yN^q$,
with $q=2-p$ for the duration exponent $p$. Both regression uncertainty and
full peak-bracket sensitivity are retained. In the completed initial scans,
the intersection of the individual allowed constant-y intervals is empty:
there is no one y compatible with every bracket. This rejects a constant
over the measured orders; it cannot exclude a later large-N limit.

Every accepted maximum has neighboring durations on both sides. No global
unimodality theorem or infinite-output-distance limit follows from these
local scans. The output prescription varies with N and is tabulated.

## Response, launch shape and exit-width tests

At the same peak-based order, separately optimized HC shares are about
0.9–2.3% below BW over the tested orders 3,4,6,8,12,16. The As-like response
is about 3.5–6.4% below BW. Thus the original small-order response envelope
does not extend unchanged to N=16. The extended As-like N=4 propagation
also shows why a claimed failure by only 0.05 percentage point is not robust:
its peak and uncertainty depend on the accepted output checkpoint.

Gaussian shares are about 7–12% larger than sech shares at equal peak-based
N. The Gaussian launch norm is smaller by $\sqrt\pi/2$, so the corresponding
normalized leading energies differ by only about 0.6–4.9%. These are different
comparisons. They do not establish shape independence at fixed input energy,
FWHM and laboratory length. Response-specific duration exponents and their
full bracket sensitivities are in final_position.json. The As-like central
exponent is lower than silica's, but its conservative bracket range includes
1.62; no corrected universal exponent is asserted.

The hypothesis tested was that the fitted exit sech width agrees within 15%
with the maximum of the isolated-profile overlap factor for each response.
It fails already in the BW N=4 near-optimum control: the fitted width is
21.323 fs, versus an overlap maximum of 15.558 fs. Halving dt changes the
fitted width by less than 0.00005 fs; widening the window and tightening the
step tolerance change it still less. At N=16 the BW, HC and As-like fitted
widths are approximately 25.4, 28.5 and 27.3 fs, respectively, versus overlap
maxima 15.56, 17.71 and 19.15 fs. Their full duration-bracket intervals also
miss the 15% acceptance region. FWHM-derived and area-inferred widths are
listed separately; this is not an ambiguity between a sech parameter and
intensity FWHM. Using the electronic nonlinear coefficient increases the
area-inferred width and does not rescue the equality.
The same BW N=4 control has area-inferred width 19.405 fs and
FWHM-derived sech width 21.475 fs, so the 15% equality also fails under
the width convention directly implied by the original energy corollary.

The time-rescaling field control passes. The tau2-halved overlap maximum
was also computed, but its proposed fission scans remain unrun; they are not
evidence for response universality. The executed checks are in accuracy.json
and inventory.json. These findings refute
the proposed **exit** rule. It does not exclude a transient role for response
overlap while colliding solitons exchange energy.

## Physical units and input constraints

The physical model uses the published SMF-28 fit (Boilard et al., DOI
10.1038/s41598-022-08329-3; arXiv:2109.13366; full text read in the prior
independent audit), retaining beta3 and higher orders, HC, self-steepening,
gamma0=1.3/(W km), 0.2 dB/km uniform loss and a 1550 nm carrier. This is a
specified fibre model, not a calibration of the PI's spool or wavelength-
dependent mode area. Input and output spectra are checked in 1200–2300 nm.
At the carrier, the fitted coefficients are $\beta_2=-20.09933\,{\rm ps^2/km}$
and $\beta_3=0.124670\,{\rm ps^3/km}$ (same run, independent.py and
final_metrics.json).

Four objectives are kept separate: energy share, energy at the share optimum,
best energy found with a fixed 3 nJ transform-limited sech launch and 10 kW peak-power limit over
T0=150–500 fs at L=2 m, and directly integrated photon fractions. Local-ZS
and directly gated energies are both reported. A finite constrained scan
provides a best-found value, not a proof of a global continuous optimum.

The central local-ZS share optima for N=3,4,6 give roughly 70.2%, 60.9%,
48.9% energy shares and 1.44–1.46 nJ leading energies, at output lengths
about 1.2, 2.2 and 4.7 m. Directly gated energies and separately optimized
gated-share peaks are reported alongside the ZS diagnostic in tables.md.
The local dispersion changes across the pulse bandwidth, so the local-ZS
proxy is not treated as an exact physical photon or energy measurement.
The corresponding integrated photon fractions are about 73.8%, 65.3%,
54.0%; energy fractions and photon fractions are not interchangeable.
After the frozen midpoint refinements, the full efficient-energy resolution
intervals are approximately [1.378,1.524], [1.366,1.511] and [1.382,1.528] nJ.
All lie inside the preregistered 1.35–1.55 nJ interval. These intervals keep
the fibre parameters fixed and do not represent material-parameter uncertainty.

Under the stated 3 nJ, 10 kW, 2 m constraint, the best resolved sampled
point is T0=150 fs, giving 1.808 nJ by local ZS and 1.811 nJ by direct gating,
with photon fraction 0.6686. Seven of eight sampled durations are resolved;
the 500 fs extraction is excluded for overlapping gates and inconsistent
ZS/gated energy. A boundary best-found point and a finite grid do not certify
a continuous maximum. Its 160 fs neighbor gives 1.791 nJ by direct gating.

For accepted physical points, the loss-corrected photon invariant drifts by
less than 1.5e-8. At the efficient-point brackets, at least 99.999999998% of
total energy and 99.9992% of leading gated energy lie in 1200–2300 nm.
The constrained best point has total/leading support above 99.9999998% and
99.99997%, respectively. These checks support the stated spectral model
domain; uniform loss and constant mode area remain model assumptions.

window, shooting-mesh and sampled-kernel errors; late-window contamination
is retained and excluded, not silently overwritten. The independently coded

3 fs; the independent exact-transform propagation avoids that quadrature

peak-share/position fit has been measured.
