## Restatement and assumptions

The proposed exponential rate of the temporally separated, blue radiation
energy is the smallest blue analyticity-strip width of the corresponding
TOD-free evolution. The tested core has instantaneous Kerr, cubic dispersion,
no loss or shock, and real even sech or Gaussian inputs. The Raman extension
uses the strip of its own TOD-free model. It is an asymptotic yield law, not a
ceiling and not an absolute spectral-peak formula.

Additional requirements to test: the frequency band and temporal gate must
isolate radiation; leakage and numerical roundoff must be below the signal;
the radiation must remain inside the temporal and spectral numerical windows;
the two-parameter fit at fixed strip must predict points omitted from fitting.
With shock, energy and the conserved weighted spectral norm must be reported
separately. A directional blue strip need not equal a symmetric strip.

## Independent model and cheap checks

My implementation uses the positive Fourier transform: `q=ifft(u)`,
`u=fft(q)`, with linear generator
$iD(w)=i(-w^2/2+\delta_3w^3)$. The nonlinear generator is
$iN^2(1+sw)\widehat{u[(1-f_R)|u|^2+f_Rh_R*|u|^2]}$.
The centered interaction-picture RK4 update and a separate Yoshida splitting
implementation are in run `2026-10-16-gpt-a-dw-core`. No proposer code is
imported. No spectral damping window is applied in this independent code.
The pinned agents' Julia solver is a separate implementation check.

For non-exact launches I infer the blue strip from the TOD-free Fourier
amplitude, fitting $\ln|\hat u|=a-dw$ and checking neighboring resolved
amplitude windows. The Roy model instead uses
$\ln|\hat u|=a-dw+b\ln w$ to allow an algebraic tail factor. No DW yield
enters either strip calculation. The Roy scan samples propagation every
0.005; the usual held-out scans use 0.0025, with the excluded sech N=4
scan using 0.00125. Only scalar tail diagnostics are retained along the
trajectory, not full field histories. These sampled minima and finite
frequency fits are numerical estimates, not a proof of the remote-frequency
singularity structure (`2026-10-16-gpt-a-dw-strips`,
`2026-10-16-gpt-a-dw-heldout-strips`).

The zero-dispersion edge follows from $D''(w_z)=0$:
$w_z=1/(6\delta_3)$. The leading resonance is $w_{DW}=1/(2\delta_3)$.
A Fourier amplitude $\exp(-dw)$ therefore gives a resonant **power** factor
$\exp(-d/\delta_3)$; confusing amplitude with power would give a factor-of-two
error. All quantities in this argument are dimensionless. Reflection of time
must interchange positive and negative TOD when Raman and shock are absent.

For the exact breather, solving the denominator polynomial gives
$d_*=\frac12\arccos(\sqrt3-1)$. The printed decimal on the card is slightly
wrong: the computed value is 0.37473443270874013, rather than 0.374664
(`2026-10-16-gpt-a-gate-null`). This is too small a discrepancy to affect the
card's tolerance. The independent propagator's exact-breather errors decrease
at fourth order under step halving (`2026-10-16-gpt-a-dw-core`).

For real causal Raman response, the potential multiplying $u$ remains real.
Without shock this equation conserves $\int|u|^2d\tau$. With shock, its
conserved spectral quadratic form is
$\int |\hat u(w)|^2/(1+sw)\,dw$, on the physical positive-frequency domain
when the field has negligible support outside it. This equals the physical
photon weight only for $s=1/(\omega_0T_0)$. Energy, this conserved form,
physical photon number, and negative-frequency leakage are recorded
separately. A frequency-dependent factor $F(w)$ multiplying the nonlinear
generator instead conserves the form weighted by
$[F(w)(1+sw)]^{-1}$; conservation of that artificial form does not establish
conservation of physical photons.

For the no-shock claim I reproduce its conserved envelope-norm convention.
Calling this exactly the broadband physical photon count would require an
additional approximation. At a DW resonance the relevant shock group is
$sw_{DW}\simeq s/(2\delta_3)$, so small $s$ alone does not justify dropping
shock from a far-blue radiation calculation. This distinction changes
prefactors and measured yields without automatically changing the leading
strip exponent.

## Physical scope and fine tuning

The verified quantity is an exponentially small radiation contribution of
an idealized cubic-dispersion model. It is not an optimized yield ceiling.
The sech anchor can be prepared without tuning material coefficients to an
exact-solution manifold; the independent Gaussian tests also show that
integrability of the launch is unnecessary in the tested range. But an
arbitrarily far-blue resonance is not a uniform physical limit of a cubic
dispersion expansion. If a dimensionless fourth-order coefficient is
$\delta_4=\beta_4/(24|\beta_2|T_0^2)$, neglecting it at the leading DW
frequency requires $|\delta_4|/(2\delta_3^2)\ll1$. Neglecting shock requires
$s/(2\delta_3)\ll1$ there, and a constant nonlinear coefficient requires
small fractional variation across the radiation band. These additional
groups are not controlled by making TOD small at fixed fibre parameters.
No laboratory realization or noise floor was verified in this audit.

I did not perform a new mask-adjoint search for this claim: its core is an
exponent for specified transform-limited launches, not an assertion of an
optimal mask or an upper bound. The descriptive collapse does not acquire
an optimality interpretation from this audit. The shaping claim requires
its own independent adjoint attack.

## Fixed-gate obstruction to the literal limit

Run `2026-10-16-gpt-a-gate-null` is an exact, high-precision null test.
The field is the TOD-free breather at $\xi_f=3\pi/4$; the parameter called
$\delta_3$ in that run sets only the measuring band edge. There is no DW
generation in that experiment. For the analytic two-edge gate
$$g_R(\tau)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-2(\tau-R)}}+
             \frac{1}{1+e^{2(\tau+R)}},$$
$g_R(id_*)\ne0$. Multiplying by the gate therefore leaves the closest
breather pole. Its squared Fourier tail decays as $e^{-2d_*w}$, so
integrating from $w_z=1/(6\delta_3)$ gives
$$\eta_{\rm null}\sim C_R e^{-d_*/(3\delta_3)},\qquad
  \lim_{\delta_3\to0}\delta_3\ln\eta_{\rm null}=-d_*/3.$$
The residue calculation is independently checked against direct Fourier
transforms at resolved frequencies. The null-background limit differs
from the proposed radiation limit. Moving a fixed gate farther out changes
$C_R$ but does not remove this issue
([null-test figure](../../runs/2026-10-16-gpt-a-dw-analysis/gate_null.png)).

This is **not, by itself, a full-TOD counterexample**: it does not justify
interchanging small TOD with the remote-frequency limit of the perturbed
solution. It does establish that the fixed gate does not exactly exclude
soliton spectral tails, and that finite-range agreement cannot establish
the literal limit without a uniform contamination argument. The gate also
must remain clear of a drifting Raman core and account for later radiation
events, not merely the earliest minimum-strip event.

One possible repair to investigate is a gate radius that scales with TOD.
For this exact null, the nearest-pole residue is proportional to
$e^{-2R}$ at large $R$. Setting $R=\rho/\delta_3$ changes its power exponent
to $4\rho+d_*/3$. The null becomes smaller than the proposed radiation
scale if $\rho>d_*/6$. A leading radiation ray can still clear that gate
when $\rho<(\xi_f-\xi_*)/4$. These are conditions on this null and on
leading ray separation; they are not a proof of the perturbed GNLSE law.
A proper outgoing-radiation projection is another possible repair. Both
require work before the literal asymptotic statement can receive audit
sign-off (`2026-10-16-gpt-a-gate-null`).
