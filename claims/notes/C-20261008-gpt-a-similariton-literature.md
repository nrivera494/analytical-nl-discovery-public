Hammani et al. Eq. (4), visually checked in the downloaded author PDF and checked against the passive-gain limit and the Gaussian SPM gradient, reads

$$\frac{z_{\rm WB}}g(e^{gz_{\rm WB}}-1)=\frac{T_0^2}{16\ln2\,e^{-3/2}\gamma\beta_2P_0},$$

where $T_0$ is intensity FWHM. For the intensity $P_0e^{-(T/a)^2}$, define $G_D=ga^2/\beta_2=gL_D$, $N_0^2=\gamma P_0a^2/\beta_2$ and $\tau_{\rm WB}=gz_{\rm WB}$. The same approximate boundary is

$$\tau_{\rm WB}(e^{\tau_{\rm WB}}-1)=\frac{e^{3/2}}4\frac{G_D^2}{N_0^2}.$$

This is an onset estimate for an initially unchirped Gaussian in the SPM-dominated regime, not an exact Euler shock time or an exclusion from the eventual NLSE density basin. The earlier helper's denominator $1-e^{-gz}$ omitted a compensating exponential factor and is **not used**. None of our numerical classifications was tuned with this literature formula.

Additional reduced-model prior art: Wabnitz (2007), DOI [10.1109/LPT.2007.893823](https://doi.org/10.1109/LPT.2007.893823), **abstract only**, [institutional record](https://iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/1214370), explicitly treats attraction of initially parabolic pulses with a finite-dimensional analytical model. Its deposited PDF is restricted. Wabnitz & Finot (2008), DOI [10.1364/JOSAB.25.000614](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAB.25.000614), HAL hal-00406210, **full text available; model and transition sections read** in the [author manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/43165547_Theory_of_parabolic_pulse_propagation_in_nonlinear_dispersion-decreasing_optical_fiber_amplifiers), maps varying-dispersion amplifiers to a constant-parameter model and uses the reduced parabolic dynamics to estimate entrance distance. This further rules out presenting the invariant-family attraction picture itself as new. Whether its restricted 2007 precursor writes our exact rate constants remains unverified.
