# Second-family literature check: Raman drift of local optical equilibrium in disordered GRIN fibre

## Verdict

1. **What is open: agreed.** I re-read the load-bearing sources myself from primary copies (ledger below). Every source statement the card's gap rests on checks out, with two small additions (§2). Neither my searches nor my helper's 71 queries (§4) found work that derives the Raman-induced drift of modal fractions while a local Kerr equilibrium persists, for weak disorder in a finite GRIN pulse. One new source, arXiv:2610.23061 (16 Oct 2026; local-RJ adiabatic cooling of two polarization components in single-mode fibre, no Raman), narrows the card's cooling baseline but not its Raman gap. The card's sharpest gap stands as a literature statement. I set the card to `audited`, `audited_by: [gpt, claude]`; priority 3, `literature_state: partial` and `novelty_confidence: low` are unchanged.
2. **One substantive correction, to the regime and derivation target, not to the literature.** The card's "first concrete derivation target" assumes that the fast Kerr collision operator has the three-dimensional null space $q=(1,\Omega,\epsilon_p(\Omega))$ of the joint mode–frequency theory. In the card's own suggested window (50-µm GRIN silica, picosecond pulses) this assumption fails, for reasons stated in the primary sources themselves:
   - the Raman kinetic equations the card builds on (Baudin 2022; Xu 2014) are derived for $t_c^2/|\beta_2|\ll L_{\rm NL}$;
   - Zanaglia 2026's simulations start with $L_D(t_c)=(1$–$6)\,L_{\rm NL}$;
   - their spatio-temporal resonances need frequency offsets $\sqrt{\delta\beta/\kappa}$, which in 50-µm GRIN at 1064 nm is $2\pi\times130$ THz, a 490-nm span (run `2026-10-20-claude-interactive.21-regime-screen`).

   For picosecond coherence times the temporal weak-turbulence parameter $W_t=t_c^2/(|\beta_2|L_{\rm NL})$ is $21$–$1.5\times10^3$ at 1 ps, and the disorder-broadened resonance does not resolve frequency. The relevant local equilibrium there is a spatial RJ state with time-local (or frequency-local) multipliers, not the three-multiplier joint RJ state. I corrected the card's regime table and derivation target, keeping the superseded text marked. The preliminary derivation (Part B, exploration note `explorations/2026-10-20-claude-interactive.21-raman-parity-selection.md`) gives the detail.
3. **Scope.** The narrowing serves the PI's request only in part. The card restricts itself to random-phase ensembles with $t_c\ll T_{\rm env}$. That leaves out the coherent femtosecond experiments that motivated the field: Pourbeyram 2022 used 200 fs, Mangini 2022 used 174 fs. Those are near transform-limited pulses ($t_c\sim T_{\rm env}$) that show spatial RJ distributions within 0.5–2.5 m. I recommend a separate card for that regime, after a scoping task to settle its overlap with P-010 and P-002 (§5; task created). No second card is filed here.
4. **Lead L5 (Raman as coupling to a phonon bath): correct in principle; inside the populated band it does not act in the card's regime; the fixed point belongs to the quantum card. But the same spontaneous term seeds the empty Stokes band and sets the SRS threshold, $L_{\rm th}\approx31L_{\rm NL}$, which the card must respect.** At the card's occupations ($10^{7}$–$10^{11}$ photons per mode and frequency cell) the bath-dependent spontaneous terms are $8\times10^{-12}$–$6\times10^{-8}$ of the stimulated term. The stimulated term is temperature-independent and fixes the sign of the drift: the photon centroid (total photon momentum) always moves red, and $\theta_1$ grows toward the lattice value $\hbar/k_BT=25.5$ fs, which it never approaches classically (§6).

## 5. Scope judgment

**Does the narrowing serve the PI's request?** The PI asked for a card that generalizes optical thermodynamics to ultrashort pulses in fibre with a clear thermalizing mechanism, and that addresses self-steepening and Raman corrections. The card's narrowing is correct about prior work: shock-modified RJ distributions and stationary joint kinetics are solved. Its thermalizer (weak, partially decorrelated random birefringence) has a published rate law. Two features limit how well it serves the request.

1. **The joint-equilibrium framing does not hold in the window the card proposes** (Verdict item 2; regime-screen run). Picosecond random-phase light in 50-µm GRIN has $W_t\gg1$. Kerr exchange with disorder there relaxes the *spatial* distribution at each time slice, or at each frequency, and the frequency direction is not constrained by dispersion energy at the disorder-broadened resonance. The card's question survives — how does Raman move a local Kerr equilibrium, and for how long does the reduction hold? — but its object must be a spatial RJ state with multipliers $\theta(t)$ or $\theta(\Omega)$. The three-moment version applies only where $W_t\ll1$ and $\sqrt{\delta\beta/\kappa}$ lies inside the band. In 50-µm GRIN at 1 µm that means coherence times well below $\sqrt{\lvert\beta_2\rvert L_{\rm NL}}=218$, 69 or 26 fs at 1, 10 or 70 kW, and even then no resonant frequency exchange between groups ($R_{\rm ST}\le1.5\times10^{-2}$). A window where a joint Kerr equilibrium is fast and Raman is slow, $(T_R\lvert\beta_2\rvert L_{\rm NL})^{1/3}\ll t_c\ll\sqrt{\lvert\beta_2\rvert L_{\rm NL}}$, spans only a factor 2.6–5.3 over 70–1 kW. There is no asymptotic window.
2. **The coherent femtosecond regime is excluded.** Most published pulsed thermalization data use nearly transform-limited multimode pulses: Pourbeyram 2022 (200 fs, 1040 nm, 50-µm GRIN, ≤2.5 m) and Mangini 2022 (174 fs to 7.6 ps). For these $t_c\sim T_{\rm env}$; there is no random-phase ensemble in time, and the LP01 occupation reaches its equilibrium value "after propagating a few nonlinear lengths" (Pourbeyram 2022, p. 5), far shorter than the weak-disorder kinetic length. Garnier 2019 states explicitly that its theory does not cover femtosecond cleaning. This regime is what "optical thermodynamics of ultrashort pulses" most naturally refers to, and the card does not cover it.

**Recommendation.** Keep this card, with the corrected regime, for the incoherent-pulse problem. Open a scoping task for a separate card on the coherent femtosecond regime: spectrally resolved spatial thermalization of near transform-limited multimode pulses in GRIN fibre, which invariants fix the wavelength-resolved RJ parameters, and the shock/Raman corrections. The scoping must check overlap with P-010 (finite-pulse self-cleaning, which excludes Raman initially) and P-002/P-008 (coherent multimode solitons). ## 6. Lead L5: Raman as coupling to a phonon bath

For a Stokes process $(p,\omega)\to(q,\omega')$ plus a phonon of frequency $\Omega=\omega-\omega'$ with thermal occupation $\bar n_{\rm th}(\Omega)$, the rate is proportional to

$$
n_\omega(n_{\omega'}+1)(\bar n_{\rm th}+1)-(n_\omega+1)n_{\omega'}\bar n_{\rm th}
=n_\omega n_{\omega'}+n_\omega(\bar n_{\rm th}+1)-n_{\omega'}\bar n_{\rm th}.
$$

- **Detailed balance.** The bracket vanishes for all pairs iff $n/(n+1)$ scales as $e^{-\hbar\omega/k_BT}$, i.e. for a Bose–Einstein law in frequency at the lattice temperature with a photon chemical potential (Raman conserves photon number). This is the Kennard–Stepanov structure. The lead is correct in principle.
- **High occupation.** The stimulated term $n_\omega n_{\omega'}$ has coefficient exactly 1, independent of temperature. It has no fixed point inside the band: it moves photons red until they pile up at the red edge. The bath-dependent terms are smaller by $1/n$. With $n=P/(M\hbar\omega\Delta\nu)$ this ratio is $8\times10^{-12}$–$6\times10^{-8}$ for 1–70 kW, 10–100 modes and 0.3–3 THz (run `…-regime-screen`, `occupation`).
- **Direction of $\theta_1$.** In the photon-occupation normalization the BE law reads $1/n\simeq(\hbar\omega-\mu)/k_BT$, i.e. $\theta_1^{\rm lattice}=\hbar/k_BT=25.5$ fs at 300 K. A Kerr-equilibrated pulse has $\lvert\theta_1\rvert\sim1/(n\sigma)\lll\theta_1^{\rm lattice}$. Stimulated Raman makes $\theta_1$ grow (red shift), toward the lattice value, but the classical dynamics never reaches it, so the bath only confirms the sign. The first moment of the photon spectrum decreases monotonically for any passive causal response, because $\Delta\,{\rm Im}\tilde h(\Delta)\ge0$ (exploration note, §3).
- **Literature.** Detailed-balance thermalization of photons with a phonon or vibronic bath is the dye-microcavity BEC mechanism (Kennard–Stepanov). Fibre realizations use absorption–emission in doped fibre, not Raman. Spontaneous Raman Stokes/anti-Stokes ratios are standard thermometry. The helper's findings are in §4.1. No source located here treats fibre Raman as a route to photon BE equilibrium at high occupation, where it cannot act.
- **Qualification: the spontaneous term does matter where the occupation is zero.** The bath terms are negligible inside the populated band. They are, however, the only seed of the empty Stokes band near 13 THz, and its stimulated growth produces the ordinary SRS threshold. With the card's single-oscillator response the peak power gain is $g_{\rm pk}=2\gamma f_R\,{\rm max\,Im}\tilde h\,P$, so the CW Smith length $L_{\rm th}=16/g_{\rm pk}$ is $31\,L_{\rm NL}$ at any power (run `…-regime-screen`, `srs`).
  - Where Garnier's $L_d\ll L_{\rm NL}$ holds, the weak-disorder kinetic length exceeds it: $L_{\rm kin}/L_{\rm th}=2.7$–400 across the benchmarks.
  - Pump–Stokes walk-off, $T_{\rm env}/(\lvert\beta_2\rvert\Omega_R)=0.74$ m per ps of envelope, suppresses this channel for envelopes of 1–10 ps.
  - For quasi-CW pulses, noise-seeded SRS, not the slow in-band Raman drift, is the first Raman effect. It ends the card's slow-drift regime unless $L_{\rm kin}<L_{\rm th}$.
  - This is a correction to my own first reading of lead L5 and is now on the card. The classical in-band kinetic model omits this channel unless a noise floor is included.
- **Ownership.** Any statement about the low-occupation fixed point (BE tails, spontaneous seeding, approach to $\theta_1^{\rm lattice}$) belongs to the quantum card P-20261016-claude-interactive.7-01. The classical card should cite the result only for the sign of the drift.
