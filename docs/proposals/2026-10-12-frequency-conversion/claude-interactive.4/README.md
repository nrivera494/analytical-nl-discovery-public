# Draft problem cards: limits to χ(3) frequency-conversion efficiency in fibre and guided waves

Proposer: claude-interactive.4 (family claude, model claude-fable-5-1). Task T-20261012-claude-interactive.4-01. Date 2026-10-12.

These five cards are drafts for the orchestrator's selection in answer to the PI's request of 2026-10-12 (open bounds on nonlinear frequency-conversion efficiency). They follow `problems/_TEMPLATE.md` and are kept out of `problems/` until selected. Lane: χ(3) conversion in fibre and guided waves, including noise. Not covered here: χ(2), Kerr microresonator combs, nanophotonic structural bounds, sum-rule and passivity bounds, and bounds that follow directly from the NLSE's conservation laws (the orchestrator's lane; cards 01 and 04 say where they build on it).

Literature was searched by four foreground helpers (claude-interactive.4.1 to .4.4, about 12 minutes each, web search plus arXiv and publisher pages) and by the proposer; every DOI in the cards was resolved through the Crossref API on 2026-10-12. Access levels are stated per source. No simulation runs were made; every number in the cards is a literature value with its source.

Justifications, one sentence each:

- **01.** Novelty 3: four searches found rates, maps and record yields but no bound or scaling law and no shape optimization; Roy, Bhadra & Agrawal (PRA 79, 023824 (2009), abstract verified) already report that the pure third-order-dispersion yield saturates "typically below 10%", so the open content is the law in $N\delta_3$, the shape optimum, and the physics that lifts that level to the measured 40–50%. Impact 5: it tells ultraviolet and visible dispersive-wave source builders (solid-core and hollow-core) whether a target yield is reachable, and the single-group collapse is a design rule. Tractability 3: the compression-window and recoil arguments give candidate laws, but the fission regime is not perturbative. Oracle 5: scalar GNLSE plus adjoint input optimization, nothing missing.
- **02.** Novelty 3: Corney & Drummond (2001) derived the Raman timing jitter of solitons and explicitly left out the drift; the drift–diffusion ratio, its minimization and the efficiency trade-off are unaddressed. Impact 3: a floor below the measured pump-transfer jitter of tunable soliton sources, with a temperature signature. Tractability 5: linear soliton perturbation theory with one kernel. Oracle 4: stochastic and linearized Raman noise exist, with two recorded normalization caveats.
- **04.** Novelty 3: no bound on blue-band yield exists in print, but the ingredients are known and the orchestrator's momentum identity is one of them. Impact 3: an absolute ceiling for blue extension, where the literature has only enhancement factors. Tractability 4: an inequality chain plus an integrated Gordon law. Oracle 5.
- **03.** Novelty 2: for a fixed pump configuration the bound is a corollary of Korsgaard, Koefoed & Rottwitt (2024) and Friis, Mejling & Rottwitt (2017); only the envelope over configurations, the spontaneous-four-wave-mixing crossover and the pulsed per-mode statement are open. Impact 4: quantum-network interfaces need the floor. Tractability 5. Oracle 4.
- **05.** Novelty 1: Mei et al. (arXiv:2608.13288, 2026, full text) give the fixed-mismatch depleted ceiling $u_{\max}=1-|D_h|/2$ for the cognate multipass-cell system, and it transfers to the reduced fibre model unchanged; only the depletion-dependent mismatch, walk-off and order competition remain. Impact 3: GRIN multicolour sources. Tractability 4: a quasi-phase-matched three-wave system with a classical exact solution. Oracle 4: the 3D GRIN solver exists; a $z$-dependent coefficient may need a small opt-in solver change.

## Top three

1. **01, dispersive-wave yield law.** The lane's best combination of an open gap, a measurable low-dimensional law and a ready refutation engine. The literature has an exponentially small perturbative rate for a lone soliton, a numerical saturation level near 10% for the pure third-order-dispersion model (Roy et al. PRA 2009, abstract), and a scatter of record yields from 0.5% to 50%, with no law connecting them. Two structural arguments (the compressed-pulse spectral tail at the fission point and the recoil switch-off) each predict a law in the single group $N\delta_3$; the adjoint optimizer can try to beat it. A lab can test the collapse with one fibre by sweeping energy and pump wavelength.
2. **02, SSFS jitter floor.** The physics is one step from print: Corney & Drummond's reservoir jitter and Gordon's drift are moments of the same Raman kernel, and nobody has taken the ratio. That ratio, if design-independent, is a fluctuation–dissipation floor with a clean temperature signature, and it sets the efficiency–shift–jitter frontier of a device class that many laboratories use. Low risk of refutation on tractability; the risk is that the derivation is short, so the card's value rests on the minimization over dispersion profiles and the frontier.
3. **04, blue-band Raman budget.** It turns the orchestrator's conservation identity into a usable ceiling by adding the sign of the Raman shift, the reach law and the trapped-wave group-velocity link. It is the natural companion to 01 (01 supplies the first blue photons; 04 bounds how far they can be pushed). Rank it below 02 because its tightness is unknown and part of its content belongs to the orchestrator's lane; merge if the orchestrator's card already uses the Raman sign.

Card 03 is included because the PI asked for noise-limited conversion and the frontier is immediately useful, but its fixed-configuration core is already in print; the orchestrator should select it only if the envelope and the pulsed statement are judged enough. Card 05 is the weakest on novelty (its fixed-mismatch ceiling is already in print for a cognate system, read in full) and is offered for the multimode slot only if the orchestrator wants a GRIN card.

## Ideas dropped as already solved or out of scope

- **Third-harmonic generation in fibre and microfibre.** Phase matching and the modal overlap are solved (Efimov et al., Opt. Express 11, 2567 (2003), DOI 10.1364/OE.11.002567; Grubsky & Savchenko, Opt. Express 13, 6798 (2005), DOI 10.1364/OPEX.13.006798; Grubsky & Feinberg, Opt. Commun. 274, 447 (2007), DOI 10.1016/j.optcom.2007.02.023; all `not accessed` or `abstract only`), and nonlinear-phase-mismatch compensation with a modified coherence length is treated by Jiang, Zhang, Lee & Brambilla, Opt. Lett. 43, 2728 (2018), DOI 10.1364/OL.43.002728 (`abstract only`). A closed-form depleted ceiling with Kerr mismatch was not located, but Grubsky & Savchenko's "rigorous theory" may hold it and the impact (percent-level efficiencies) is low. Dropped.
- **Depleted conversion efficiency of continuous-wave four-wave mixing and phase conjugation.** Exact elliptic solutions with pump depletion: Cappellini & Trillo, JOSA B 8, 824 (1991), DOI 10.1364/JOSAB.8.000824 (`abstract only`). The Raman-limited noise figure of phase-insensitive and phase-sensitive parametric amplifiers: Voss & Kumar, Opt. Lett. 29, 445 (2004), DOI 10.1364/OL.29.000445 (`abstract only`); Voss, Köprülü & Kumar, JOSA B 23, 598 (2006), DOI 10.1364/JOSAB.23.000598 (`full text`). Dropped; the translator case survives as card 03.
- **Temporal-mode selectivity of frequency conversion.** Single-stage limit and multi-stage cure: Reddy, Raymer, McKinstrie, Mejling & Rottwitt, Opt. Express 21, 13840 (2013), DOI 10.1364/OE.21.013840; Reddy, Raymer & McKinstrie, Opt. Lett. 39, 2924 (2014), DOI 10.1364/OL.39.002924; time-ordering corrections: Quesada & Sipe, Opt. Lett. 41, 364 (2016), DOI 10.1364/OL.41.000364 (DOIs verified; `not accessed`). Named as known in the PI's brief. Dropped.
- **Cascaded Raman conversion to a target Stokes order (continuous wave).** Rate-equation theory and simulation of nth-order cascaded Raman fibre lasers: Jackson & Muir, JOSA B 18, 1297 (2001), DOI 10.1364/JOSAB.18.001297 (`not accessed`). The single-pass quantum limit $\omega_s/\omega_p$ is the Manley–Rowe statement. Dropped as solved or trivial.
- **Probe frequency conversion at a soliton "event horizon".** The probe obeys a linear scattering problem on the soliton's cross-phase-modulation barrier, with reflection coefficients known in closed form and Raman deceleration treated: Demircan, Amiranashvili & Steinmeyer, Phys. Rev. Lett. 106, 163901 (2011), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.106.163901; Robertson & Leonhardt, Phys. Rev. A 81, 063835 (2010), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.81.063835 (DOIs verified; `not accessed`). Dropped.
- **Walk-off-limited pulsed intermodal four-wave mixing and random-coupling penalties.** Penalty regimes are published (Xiao et al., Opt. Express 22, 32039 (2014), DOI 10.1364/OE.22.032039, `abstract only`; Guasoni et al., J. Lightwave Technol. 35, 5296 (2017), DOI 10.1109/JLT.2017.2767103, `abstract only`), and a walk-off ceiling is device optimization rather than a bound. Dropped in favour of card 05.
- **Gas-filled hollow-core ultraviolet generation as a separate card.** No bound beyond card 01 was found to be open; ionization is outside the solver, and P-009 is parked. The hollow-core yields are used as achievable points in card 01.
- **Kerr phase noise from pump quantum fluctuations as a conversion noise channel.** An order-of-magnitude estimate (phase variance of order $2\gamma PL/N_{\rm pump}$) makes it negligible; kept only as a sub-question in card 03.
